I study how smallholder farmers decide, adapt and respond to risk, and I use econometrics and explainable machine learning to turn survey data into evidence that agricultural agencies can act on.

My work sits between agricultural economics, behavioral modeling and applied data science. I am a PhD researcher at the Asian Institute of Technology, supported by the World Bank PARTNER Fellowship, and I work as an Assistant Director at the Bangladesh Agricultural Development Corporation, where I joined in 2022.

Research

Farmer behavior and technology adoption

I ask why farmers adopt a practice, how far they take it and whether they stay satisfied. I pair econometric models such as Double Hurdle and PLS-SEM with explainable machine learning, so results show both the size of an effect and the reason behind it.

Climate adaptation and livelihood vulnerability

Much of this work is in the haor wetlands and coastal belt of Bangladesh, where floods, cyclones and erosion shape what households can do. I study how communities adapt and which households stay most exposed.

Food security and the everyday environment

This strand covers food security among women informal workers in Khulna, household willingness to pay for cleaner urban air, and the psychology behind low carbon digital habits among university students.

Prices, trade and productivity

My newer work looks at rice price shocks and structural breaks, the role of trade openness, and agricultural productivity across South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa.
